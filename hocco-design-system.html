Glass carries structure.
Solid carries consequence.

A glassmorphic reworking of the visual system in §8 of the product spec. The colour roles survive unchanged — they were reasoned from user conditioning, not from style. What changes is the material: surfaces became translucent and layered, and one hard rule was added to keep that safe.

01The governing rule

Glassmorphism has one structural conflict with this product. §8 requires that risk is never carried by colour alone — the signal must survive greyscale, colour-vision deficiency, and a cheap monitor. But a translucent surface takes its final colour from whatever happens to be behind it. Saturation drops, contrast becomes unpredictable, and the exact thing the spec asks to be reliable becomes the thing that varies most.

So: glass is for structure, never for state. Depth, layering, chrome and grouping are glass. Every risk signal — the rail, the chip, the icon, the reason text, every primary action — is rendered on an opaque element. Nothing you need to trust is translucent.

Two consequences follow. Primary actions are solid raspberry rather than tinted glass, so an action can never be mistaken for chrome. And every glass surface has an opaque fallback under prefers-reduced-transparency and where backdrop-filter is unsupported — the layouts are identical, only the material changes.

Raised means touchable

The second rule, and the one glassmorphism makes easiest to break. Because every surface is made of the same material, a read-only panel and a tappable row can end up visually identical — which is exactly what happened to the first build of the summary strip.

Lift is an affordance, not decoration. If a surface is raised — outer shadow, lit top edge, its own rounded pane — it must do something when touched. Read-only surfaces are inset instead: recessed, no shadow, no gaps, hairline dividers, one continuous object.

So the summary strip is a single carved well rather than three floating cards, and cursor: pointer plus the press animation are scoped to a.row, button.row and [role="button"].row — a static row keeps its tier styling but makes no promise it can't keep.

07State chips

Always opaque, always carrying a word. Chips are labels, never controls. They were outlined pills — the universal shape of a button or a dismissible filter chip — which invited taps they can't honour. They are now flat tags: 5px radius against the button's 14px, tinted fill instead of an outline, no shadow and no press state. The tappable thing is the row around them.

Queued is not Blocked. Queued means the owning department is working through other things — contention, not failure. Conflating them sends leadership chasing the wrong fix, so they never share a colour or a shape.

08Actions

Primary actions are solid. This is the one place where refusing glass is a usability decision rather than an accessibility one: a translucent button sits in the same visual register as chrome, and the most important interaction in the product — flagging a blocker — cannot be allowed to look like furniture.

Ghost buttons are glass — they are secondary by design, and reading as chrome is the point.

10Motion

Spent only where it means something. Two moments earn it:

The Done settle — a completed row dims and gains an Undo rather than vanishing. A claim of completion is not closure.

The unblock transition — the highest-value moment in the product, the one that beats the spreadsheet. A Zone-2 row becoming actionable lifts and gains a green rail, carrying a brief trace of the wait. Prominent, never modal.

12Filtering & launch-health

One filter component (UI.filters) serves every list screen — My Work, Portfolio, Blockers. A trigger opens a thumb-zone bottom sheet with three single-select facets — brand, category, priority — that apply live as you tap. Active facets appear as removable pills in the bar. The trigger, pills and options all wear the outlined-pill control shape, never the flat tag shape — filtering is an action, so it must look like one.

Priority is the CEO's computed ordering from launch date (spec §5), so the facet's values are High / Medium / Low — plus Awaiting for the unprioritised, which have no priority yet by design.

Calendar launch-health

Every launch on the Calendar carries a three-way status — on track / delayed / blocked — that must not collapse into one dot. It's a coloured rail and an iconned label, so the signal survives greyscale; blocked is a solid fill while the others are tinted, so fill weight separates them before hue does. In dark mode the palette's two ambers sit too close, so the Calendar widens the gap itself — gold for delayed, burnt orange for blocked — driven from the shared --launch-* tokens the rails, chips and legend all read from.

13Open

Untested. The glass ground is calibrated on a good screen. It needs checking on the cheap Android handsets the reps actually carry, in a factory in daylight — that is the real contrast test, not a simulator. If it fails there, the fallback path already exists: force the opaque token set and nothing else changes.

Also unresolved from §10 of the spec: whether Portfolio should lead with at-risk cards or a tighter summary line. Both are on the Portfolio screen now — the strip sits above the band — so the first testing round can settle it rather than the armchair.
